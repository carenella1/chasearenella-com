How Do You Run a Decision Log?

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 66 seconds

Learn how to run a decision log that captures your reasoning before results are known. See a simple five-minute template, which decisions to log, and how to make reviews actually happen.

The full written guide: How Do You Run a Decision Log?

Transcript

Run a decision log by writing down each meaningful decision when you make it, then setting a date to review it. Memory rewrites history. A month later, people remember the reasoning that matches the outcome, not the reasoning they used. A log captures your thinking before the result is known, so you can judge the decision on its merits instead of its luck.

It also shows new teammates why things are the way they are. Keep each entry short enough to write in five minutes. Note who decided and when, the decision in one sentence, the real alternatives you rejected, and your main reason. Then add kill criteria: the result that would make you reverse it.

If you'd have to explain it in a meeting six months from now, log it today. A log full of trivia gets ignored. Put the review date on a calendar immediately. When it arrives, ask what happened, whether the kill criteria triggered, and what you'd do differently with the same information.

That separates a bad decision from a good one with a bad outcome. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
