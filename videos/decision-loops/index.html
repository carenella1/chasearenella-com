Decision Loops: Close the Cycle Faster

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 69 seconds

Learn what a decision loop is and why the team that closes it faster adapts faster. See where loops really slow down and three structural changes that tighten them without rushing.

The full written guide: Decision Loops

Transcript

A decision loop is the cycle you run to make any choice: notice what changed, decide, act, and learn from the result. The team that closes its loop faster adapts faster. Military strategists call it the OODA loop. Agile teams call it a sprint.

A loop closes quickly and compounds, or drags and goes stale. Most teams think their problem is deciding. The delay hides elsewhere. Signals arrive late in dashboards nobody opens.

Handoffs add days to a one-hour task. Often nobody checks the result, so the loop never closes. Speed comes from removing waiting, not from thinking less. Time each step of one recent decision.

The slowest step is rarely the one people complain about. Push decisions to whoever is closest to the information. Agree in advance on what result would change your mind. And schedule the review when you make the decision.

Strong gamers work the same way. They pre-load responses to common situations and save attention for what's new. Write down your weekly decisions and give them default answers. That frees the loop for real thought.
