Feedback Latency: Why Fast Signals Beat Perfect Ones

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 69 seconds

Learn what feedback latency is and why a fast, rough signal beats a slow, perfect one. See the warning signs of slow feedback and practical ways to shorten the gap.

The full written guide: Feedback Latency

Transcript

Feedback latency is the time between an action and the moment you learn whether it worked. Feedback quality matters, but timing matters more. A rough signal tomorrow beats a perfect one next quarter. Every day you wait, you keep making decisions.

If the first action was wrong, you stack more work on top of it. Slow feedback doesn't just delay the lesson. It multiplies the cost of the mistake. How do you spot it?

Retrospectives discuss problems nobody remembers clearly. Performance reviews surprise people. Projects look green until the week they turn red. The team argues about causes, because too much happened in between.

To shorten the gap, move reviews closer to the work. A short weekly check beats a long quarterly one, while details are fresh and fixes are cheap. Ship smaller. Two days of work teaches you something in two days.

Pick one leading indicator per goal that predicts next quarter, and check it every week. Games teach fast because feedback is instant, and strong players review replays. Borrow that habit. Review the week, not just the quarter.
