Signal vs. Noise

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 68 seconds

Learn a simple test for telling signal from noise and why noise grabs attention by default. See where AI filtering helps, where it backfires, and how to keep your filter from becoming a blindfold.

The full written guide: Signal vs. Noise

Transcript

Signal is information that should change what you do, and noise is information that feels important but shouldn't change anything. The simplest test is a question. If this were true, would I act differently? If not, it's noise, however loud it is.

A metric that always dips on Mondays is noise. A complaint that matches three others from the same week is signal. Noise wins by default. It's more frequent, more visible, and more emotional.

The meaningful change arrives quietly, once, inside something that looks routine. Without a deliberate filter, attention follows volume. AI tools can summarize a week of updates, group feedback by theme, and flag numbers that break their usual range. That frees human attention for judgment.

But over-filtering is the risk. A filter trained on what usually matters hides what has never mattered before, and new problems often first look like outliers. So keep a small raw sample in view, and audit the filter on a schedule. Ask what it dropped last month.

A filter nobody audits slowly becomes a blindfold. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
