One-Way and Two-Way Door Decisions

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 67 seconds

Learn how to sort decisions into one-way and two-way doors based on how easily they can be reversed. See how to match the right amount of process to each choice and avoid common sorting mistakes.

The full written guide: One-Way and Two-Way Door Decisions

Transcript

A one-way door decision is hard or costly to undo, while a two-way door decision can be reversed cheaply, and knowing which is which tells you how much process each choice deserves. Most decisions are two-way doors. Yet teams often treat them as permanent, adding meetings and sign-offs that a quick test would settle. Spend process only where it buys something.

To sort a choice, ask what it costs to undo in money, time, and trust. Ask how long until you would know it was wrong. Then try to shrink it into a smaller, reversible version first. For two-way doors, name one owner, set a short deadline, and note what would make you reverse.

Then act. A wrong call caught in a week costs less than a right call reached in a month. For one-way doors, slow down on purpose. Gather outside views, ask someone to argue the opposite, and record your reasoning.

Fear can make a reversible choice feel permanent. A small pilot can become permanent once customers depend on it. And reversible never means endlessly revisited, so set a review date. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
