What Is Systems Thinking for Teams?

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 71 seconds

Learn how systems thinking helps teams see results as the product of structure rather than individual effort. Discover four areas to examine, how feedback loops work, and a simple first exercise to try.

The full written guide: What Is Systems Thinking for Teams?

Transcript

Systems thinking for teams means treating results as the output of a structure, not just individual effort. When a team keeps missing deadlines, the instinct is to find who dropped the ball. A better question is this. What about the way work flows here makes missed deadlines likely?

Usually the answer is structural. Work arrives faster than it can be finished. Priorities change mid-sprint. Approvals sit in one person's queue.

People tend to produce the results their system is designed for. To start, look at how work flows in and out, how fast feedback arrives, what really gets rewarded, and the one bottleneck limiting everything. Then notice your feedback loops. A reinforcing loop amplifies itself, so trust earns autonomy and better shipping, or oversight slows a team into more missed deadlines.

A balancing loop pushes back, like burnout after a hard push. Pick one recurring problem. Write down the last three times it happened and what came just before. Look for the common structural cause, not the common person.
