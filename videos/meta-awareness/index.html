Meta Awareness: Spotting When the Best Strategy Shifts

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 69 seconds

Learn what a meta is, why every field has one, and the early signs that the accepted best strategy is starting to shift. See how to build the habit of adapting early, including how leaders can hear from frontline teams.

The full written guide: Meta Awareness

Transcript

Meta awareness is noticing when your field's accepted best strategy starts to shift, so you adapt before everyone else does. Gamers call the dominant way to play the meta, short for most effective tactics available. Notice a change late, and you keep running a strategy that has quietly stopped working. Every field has a meta, from sales teams to hiring engineers.

It feels like common sense, which is why the change is hard to see. Shifts start as small anomalies. A reliable playbook gives mixed results. A small competitor wins with an approach everyone dismissed.

Your best people quietly work around the process. Costs rise while results stay flat. To build the habit, watch the edges, not the center. Track which experiments from newer players keep working after the novelty fades.

Write down why you believe your current approach is best. When those reasons stop being true, the strategy is on borrowed time. Leaders often see shifts last, so give frontline teams a low-risk way to report changes, and treat their workarounds as data, not disobedience. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
