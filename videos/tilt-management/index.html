Tilt Management: Stop One Mistake From Becoming Five

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 64 seconds

Learn what tilt is, where the term comes from, and how frustration from one setback quietly worsens the next decisions. See the early warning signs and simple reset habits leaders can use to keep a team steady.

The full written guide: Tilt Management

Transcript

Tilt is when frustration from one bad outcome leaks into your next decisions and makes them worse. Poker players coined it for someone who loses a big hand, then plays recklessly to win it back. Gamers saw the same pattern. At work, a leader takes a hard hit in a morning meeting, then makes three snap calls before lunch.

Tilt is easiest to stop early. Notice when you're replaying the last mistake instead of reading the current situation. Notice speed, bigger risks, short replies, and blame. Good players build a reset into their routine, like a breath, a pause, or a fixed phrase.

What matters is that it's automatic, not dependent on willpower. At work, try a rule: no irreversible decisions within an hour of a major setback. And name it out loud. Saying we're a bit tilted gives everyone permission to slow down.

Leaders set the emotional tempo. Reset visibly, and your team learns a bad outcome is information, not an emergency. Treat tilt as a skill you practice and review. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
