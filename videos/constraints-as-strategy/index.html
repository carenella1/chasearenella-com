Constraints as Strategy

The full written guide: Constraints as Strategy

Transcript

Using constraints as strategy means deliberately limiting your options, because a limit forces clearer choices and faster execution than open-ended flexibility. Keeping options open feels safe. But every open option has a cost. It drains attention and coordination, and delays commitment.

A team with ten priorities has none. When the choice set is small, decisions get faster, trade-offs become visible, and people can focus long enough to finish. The best constraints are simple enough to remember and strict enough to bite. Try a work-in-progress limit for each person.

Try fixed timeboxes, where scope flexes but the date doesn't. Pick one priority per quarter. Everything else is maintenance. Keep a stop list of things the team agrees not to do.

Limits don't kill creativity. They direct it. Like tight rules in a game, a fixed budget or deadline pushes people toward the essential version of an idea. Choose constraints on purpose, because accidental ones frustrate.

A constraint is a tool, not a value. If it causes workarounds or quiet resentment, loosen it deliberately. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
