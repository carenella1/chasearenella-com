Which Decisions Should Stay Human When You Use AI?

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 71 seconds

Learn a four-question test for deciding which choices a person should own and which AI can prepare or handle. See why people decisions stay human and how to make your team's lines visible.

The full written guide: Which Decisions Should Stay Human When You Use AI?

Transcript

Keep a decision human when it involves accountability, values trade-offs, unfamiliar situations, or consequences that are hard to reverse. Let AI handle the preparation. Here is a simple test. Ask whether someone will need to explain this decision to the people it affects.

Ask whether it trades one thing people care about against another. Ask whether the situation is unlike anything a model has seen. And ask whether a mistake would be expensive or impossible to undo. If any answer is yes, a person should own the call.

AI can still lay out options you missed, summarize what's known, argue the other side of your plan, and draft the message that explains the outcome. For routine, reversible, low-stakes choices, like scheduling, letting AI decide is often fine. People decisions are different. Hiring, promotions, performance concerns, and conflicts stay human.

Teams notice who actually decided, and trust drops fast when a decision feels outsourced. Make the line visible. Write down which decisions are AI-assisted and which are AI-decided, share the list, and revisit it every few months. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
