Blameless Retrospectives

A video by Chase Arenella · November 25, 2026 · 67 seconds

Learn how blameless retrospectives separate accountability from blame and why punishment makes reviews dishonest. See a repeatable structure for finding system fixes that actually stick.

The full written guide: Blameless Retrospectives

Transcript

A blameless retrospective asks how the system allowed a failure, not who caused it. Blameless does not mean nobody is accountable. Accountability asks who will own the fix. Blame asks who should feel bad.

If one person's slip can cause a failure, the system lacked a safeguard. When reviews end in punishment, people hide things. They soften timelines and stay quiet about near misses. A gaming squad that dreads replay review stops calling out misplays, and errors repeat.

Lower the cost of saying I made that call, and you get accurate information sooner. Hold the review within a week, for sixty to ninety minutes. Build a timeline from logs before interpreting it. Ask what you saw, not why you did that.

Look for contributing factors across process, tools, and information. Write fixes aimed at safeguards, each with one owner and one deadline. Watch your language, because words like careless turn a review into a verdict. Leaders should speak last and go first on their own mistakes.

Then check the actions at the next review. More from Chase Arenella at chase arenella dot com.
